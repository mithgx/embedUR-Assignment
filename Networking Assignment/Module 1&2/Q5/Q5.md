When duplicate IP addresses are configured in a network, two or more devices share the same IP address, which breaks the fundamental rule that each device must have a unique identifier.

At the ARP (Address Resolution Protocol) level, devices continuously update their ARP tables with conflicting mappings (same IP mapped to different MAC addresses). This causes instability, as packets may be sent to different devices at different times.

From a user perspective, this results in intermittent connectivity issues such as random disconnections, inability to access services, or inconsistent network behavior.

At the network level, switches and routers cannot reliably forward packets due to inconsistent IP-to-MAC mappings. Some systems may detect the conflict and disable one of the devices or generate warnings.

Overall, duplicate IP addresses lead to network instability, packet loss, and service disruption, making proper IP management essential.

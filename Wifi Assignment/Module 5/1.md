# Wi-Fi 6, 6E, and 7 vs Wi-Fi 5

Wi-Fi standards have evolved to improve throughput, efficiency, and performance in dense environments.

Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac)

* Operates only in 5 GHz
* Uses OFDM and 256-QAM
* Supports MU-MIMO (downlink only)
* Focuses mainly on higher peak data rates

Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax)

* Operates in 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz
* Introduces OFDMA and 1024-QAM
* Supports uplink + downlink MU-MIMO
* Adds Target Wake Time (TWT)
* Improves efficiency in dense networks rather than just peak speed

Wi-Fi 6E

* Extension of Wi-Fi 6 into 6 GHz band
* Provides large number of clean channels
* Eliminates legacy interference
* Enables wider channels (80/160 MHz consistently)

Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be)

* Operates in 2.4, 5, and 6 GHz
* Introduces 4096-QAM
* Supports Multi-Link Operation (MLO)
* Channel widths up to 320 MHz
* Extremely high throughput and ultra-low latency

Key differences:

* Wi-Fi 5 → speed-focused
* Wi-Fi 6 → efficiency + capacity
* Wi-Fi 6E → new spectrum (clean environment)
* Wi-Fi 7 → extreme throughput + multi-link performance

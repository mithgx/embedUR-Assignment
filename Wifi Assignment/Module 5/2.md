# Role of OFDMA in Wi-Fi 6

OFDMA (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access) is a key enhancement in Wi-Fi 6 that improves spectrum utilization.

In traditional OFDM (Wi-Fi 5), the entire channel is allocated to one user at a time. In OFDMA, the channel is divided into smaller units called **Resource Units (RUs)**, allowing multiple users to transmit or receive simultaneously.

How it improves efficiency:

* **Parallel transmissions** → multiple users served in one time slot
* **Reduced contention** → fewer collisions and backoff delays
* **Lower latency** → especially for small packets (IoT, VoIP)
* **Better performance in dense environments** → stadiums, campuses

Example:
A 20 MHz channel can be split into multiple RUs (e.g., 26-tone, 52-tone), enabling simultaneous communication with multiple devices.

OFDMA shifts Wi-Fi from a contention-heavy system to a more scheduled and efficient access mechanism.

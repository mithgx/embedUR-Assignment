# Difference between 802.11a and 802.11b

802.11a and 802.11b are early Wi-Fi standards defined by IEEE 802.11, differing mainly in frequency band, speed, and interference characteristics.

802.11a operates in the 5 GHz band and supports higher data rates up to 54 Mbps using OFDM modulation. It experiences less interference due to fewer overlapping devices but has shorter range due to higher frequency.

802.11b operates in the 2.4 GHz band with a maximum data rate of 11 Mbps using DSSS modulation. It has better range but suffers from significant interference due to congestion in the 2.4 GHz band (Bluetooth, microwaves, etc.).

Overall, 802.11a offers higher speed and lower interference, while 802.11b provides better coverage but lower performance.

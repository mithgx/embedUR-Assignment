# DSSS and FHSS

DSSS (Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum) and FHSS (Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum) are spread spectrum techniques used in early wireless communication systems.

DSSS
In DSSS, each data bit is multiplied with a pseudo-random chip sequence, spreading the signal over a wider frequency band. At the receiver, the same sequence is used to recover the original signal.

* Provides resistance to interference and noise
* Used in 802.11b (with CCK encoding)
* Better throughput compared to FHSS

FHSS
In FHSS, the signal rapidly hops between different frequency channels in a predefined sequence known to both transmitter and receiver.

* Avoids interference by not staying on one frequency
* More robust in noisy environments
* Lower data rates compared to DSSS

Comparison:

* DSSS → higher data rate, continuous spectrum
* FHSS → better interference avoidance, but lower efficiency

Modern Wi-Fi no longer uses FHSS and has moved to OFDM-based techniques.

# Significance of OFDM in WLAN

OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) is a key PHY-layer technique used in modern Wi-Fi standards (802.11a/g/n/ac/ax). It divides a high-rate data stream into multiple lower-rate subcarriers that are transmitted in parallel over different frequencies.

This improves performance in several ways:

* **Resilience to multipath fading**: Since each subcarrier has a lower symbol rate, inter-symbol interference (ISI) is reduced.
* **Higher spectral efficiency**: Subcarriers are orthogonal, allowing them to overlap without interference, maximizing bandwidth usage.
* **Adaptive modulation**: Different modulation schemes can be applied depending on channel conditions.
* **Better throughput**: Parallel transmission increases overall data rates.

OFDM is a major shift from earlier techniques (like DSSS) and is fundamental to achieving high data rates in modern WLANs.

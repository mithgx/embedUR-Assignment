# Modulation Schemes in Wi-Fi PHY Layer

Modulation in the PHY layer determines how digital data is encoded onto radio waves. It directly impacts data rate, reliability, and signal robustness.

Basic Working:
Data bits are mapped to symbols using amplitude, phase, or frequency variations of the carrier signal.

Common Modulation Schemes:

| Modulation | Bits/Symbol | Used In          | Characteristics                     |
| ---------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| BPSK       | 1           | Early OFDM (11a) | Very robust, low data rate          |
| QPSK       | 2           | 11a/g/n          | Balanced performance                |
| 16-QAM     | 4           | 11a/g/n          | Higher rate, moderate robustness    |
| 64-QAM     | 6           | 11n/ac           | High throughput, needs good SNR     |
| 256-QAM    | 8           | 11ac             | Very high speed, less robust        |
| 1024-QAM   | 10          | 11ax             | Maximum throughput, high SNR needed |

Performance Comparison:

* Lower-order modulation (BPSK, QPSK) → better reliability, works at low signal strength
* Higher-order modulation (256/1024-QAM) → higher throughput but requires high SNR and low interference
* Wi-Fi dynamically adapts modulation using rate adaptation algorithms based on channel conditions

Modern standards (802.11n/ac/ax) combine modulation with MIMO and OFDM/OFDMA to maximize efficiency and throughput.
